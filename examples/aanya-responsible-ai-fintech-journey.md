## Week 6 - Ship the capstone

## Capstone: FairLend Lens

### Context

Credit-risk models can affect access to financial products. Aanya wanted to learn what responsible evaluation looks like before treating a prediction as a decision.

### Challenge

Accuracy alone cannot answer whether outcomes are equitable, understandable, or safe to use. Public datasets also have limitations and may not represent real lending populations.

### Approach

1. Use a synthetic or openly licensed educational dataset.
2. Train a simple baseline and report standard performance metrics.
3. Compare selected group-level outcome measures.
4. Generate a plain-language explanation for a sample prediction.
5. Document assumptions, limitations, and questions a real review team would need to answer.

### Outcome

The project produces an evaluation report and a short demo. Its value is a transparent learning artifact: it shows the checks, trade-offs, and limits that are easy to hide behind a single model score.

### 90-second pitch

> I'm Aanya, a final-year Computer Science student at DTU. I am interested in responsible AI for fintech because automated financial decisions can have real consequences for people. I built FairLend Lens, an educational prototype that evaluates a simple credit-risk model alongside group-level checks and plain-language explanations. Building it taught me that fairness is not one number and that a model cannot be trusted without understanding the data, trade-offs, and people affected. Next, I want to learn from teams building transparent, accountable financial products.

### Sustainable habit

Every Friday, Aanya shares one short artifact: a learning note, a chart with assumptions, a project decision, or a question she is still investigating.
